The market built libraries. Agents need a dispatch room.

Traditional DAM platforms are strong at storage, metadata, brand governance, usage rights, search, approvals, and distribution. That matters after a media asset becomes official.

Agent-made media creates a different mess: the useful work lives in chats, prompts, local folders, model settings, discarded drafts, and private experiments. By the time it reaches a DAM, the production context is already gone.

Nitrate sits one step earlier. It turns a brief into an agent-readable packet, sends it to the tools creators already use, and brings the work back as reviewable media with context attached.

What a DAM becomes when the workers are agents.

Not just “upload, tag, approve.” Nitrate manages the operating objects that make AI media reproducible, reviewable, and team-owned.

Use cases that old DAM language misses.

Campaign swarm without creative drift

Review AI media like production, not archaeology
